# Using GHL's marketing side — where the real leverage is

We've been using GHL as a CRM with a calendar. That's maybe a third of it. Two
features change how this gets *sold* rather than what it does, and they're worth
more than any feature we could add to the agent.

Researched July 2026. Pricing figures are reported by third parties and vary
between sources — verify against GHL's own pricing page before quoting anyone.

---

## 1. Snapshots — this turns our build into a product 🌟

A **Snapshot** packages an entire configured sub-account — calendars, custom
fields, pipelines, workflows, email templates — into a template you can deploy
into a new sub-account in minutes.

Look at what we just spent hours doing by hand: four calendars with capacities
and hours, a pipeline with five stages, thirteen custom fields, a private
integration token. **That is a Snapshot.** Do it once, and the next hotel client
is a deploy, not a setup.

This is the answer to a problem already sitting in `TASKS.md`. The plan says
"build the HVAC and med spa versions (should be quick — mostly config)". With
Snapshots the claim gets stronger: one snapshot per vertical, each pairing with
the industry config we already have in `agent-core`.

```
roofing snapshot  + roofing.ts   -> a roofing client, live in an hour
hotel snapshot    + hotel.ts     -> a hotel client, live in an hour
hvac snapshot     + hvac.ts      -> next vertical, same day
```

The code side is already built for this — `industries/index.ts` was designed so a
vertical is a config file. Snapshots are the same idea on the GHL side. The two
together are a product; either alone is a bespoke build.

**What to do:** once the hotel account is fully configured and the demo is
recorded, save it as a Snapshot before touching anything else. It's the most
valuable artefact we'll produce and it takes minutes.

---

## 2. SaaS Mode / rebilling — recurring revenue instead of project fees 🌟

GHL's SaaS Mode lets an agency white-label the whole platform — own subdomain,
own logo, own pricing tiers — and resell sub-accounts.

The economics reported by third parties: a flat monthly platform fee to GHL
(reported around $297–497/mo depending on plan) regardless of how many
sub-accounts run under it, with agencies typically charging clients $97–497/mo
each. That's the difference between selling a build once and owning a recurring
line.

Why it matters to us specifically: **the AI agent is the differentiator that
makes a rebilled GHL account worth paying for.** Plenty of agencies resell GHL;
almost none can say "and it answers your phone, qualifies the lead and books
them in while they're still talking." That's a genuinely defensible reason to
charge at the top of that range rather than the bottom.

This is a business decision, not an engineering one — but it should shape the
Upwork positioning on Day 10. "I'll build you a bot" is a one-off fee. "Your
front desk never misses a call, $X/month" is a business.

---

## 3. Attribution — the thing that actually closes a sale

GHL's attribution reporting tracks the full journey from first touch to closed
deal: which ad, which social post, which page brought in the customer who booked.

Right now `TASKS.md` plans a dashboard showing conversations, bookings and
conversion rate. Attribution makes it much stronger, because it answers the only
question a buyer really has:

> "This Facebook ad cost $340. It produced 22 calls, our agent booked 9 of them,
> and 4 became customers."

That's the Day 10 case study, generated by the platform rather than assembled by
hand. Worth wiring the agent's outcome and lead score back into GHL properly
(already on the task list) so attribution has something to attribute *to*.

---

## 4. Missed-call text-back — pairs perfectly with the voice agent

Described in every review as the single stickiest feature for local service
businesses: a call goes unanswered, GHL immediately texts "sorry we missed you,
how can we help?"

Ours can be better than the standard version. Instead of a canned text:

- the agent calls back, or
- it texts *with context* — and can then book over SMS

For a roofer on a roof or a hotel front desk at 2am, this is the whole pitch in
one feature. It's also the natural home for the voice work in
`VOICE_PROPOSAL.md`: get a real phone number, and missed-call recovery becomes
available at the same time.

---

## 5. Reputation / review requests — closes the loop we've already half-built

Automated review requests by text after a stay or a job, including video
testimonials. Reviews drive more inbound, which drives more calls, which is what
the agent handles.

We already have the "after booking" sequence in the email plan. A review request
is the same machinery pointed at a later moment — and our agent can handle the
*reply*, which no template-based review flow does. Someone replies "actually the
room was too hot", and instead of a dead-end autoresponder it becomes a real
conversation and a service recovery.

---

## 6. Funnels and forms — the lead capture you asked about

This is the "customer grabbing system" from the engagement plan. GHL funnels and
forms are a lead-capture surface that feeds contacts straight into a workflow —
which can then hand off to our agent.

Worth being honest about the trade-off: we've already built a better version of
the front door for our own demo site — a live conversation beats a form. Where
GHL forms win is on a *client's existing* site, or as a paid-ad landing page
where we don't control the stack. Both are useful; they're just not the demo.

---

## What I'd actually do, in order

1. **Snapshot the hotel account** once it's finished. Minutes of work, and it's
   the difference between a demo and a product. Do this before anything else.
2. **Wire outcome + lead score back into GHL** (already on the task list) so
   attribution and reporting have real data.
3. **Get a phone number**, which unlocks missed-call text-back alongside the
   voice demo.
4. **Add review requests** to the post-booking sequence, with our agent handling
   replies.
5. **Decide on SaaS Mode** before writing the Upwork positioning — recurring
   pricing and one-off pricing need different pitches.

Items 1 and 2 are hours. Item 5 is a conversation, not a build, and it's probably
worth more than the rest combined.
